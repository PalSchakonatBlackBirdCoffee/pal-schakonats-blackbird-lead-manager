# Who Blackbird sells to

Source: Blackbird onboarding deck V3, blackbirdcoffee.dk/se, and booking calls.

## The product, in one line

Machine, coffee and service in one subscription at a fixed price per cup, roughly 3 to 5 SEK.
No purchase price for the machine, no start fee. The machine reports its own consumption and
faults, so refills arrive before the customer runs out.

## The segment

The deck splits the market three ways and Blackbird deliberately targets the middle:

| | 1-4 users | **5-50 users** | 51+ users |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market size | 20m | **15m** | 1m |
| Current solution | 99% consumer-grade | **80% consumer-grade** | 95% professional |
| Coverage | well served | **under-served** | well served |

The middle band is the opportunity: enough people to consume real volume, but still drinking out
of a home-grade filter brewer or a supermarket espresso machine that nobody services. The lead
filter is 3 to 50 (see `REQUIREMENTS.md`), which starts one step below the deck's band.

Consumption threshold from the deck: decision makers at companies using more than 2 kg of coffee
a month.

## What they are replacing

Filter brewer, about 35% of the market. Consumer espresso machine, about 30%. Capsules, about 5%.
All three are cheap per cup on paper, 2.28 to 3.67 SEK, so Blackbird is not a saving, it is a
small step up in cost for a large step up in quality. The deck frames the whole upgrade as
peanuts against payroll.

Signals a company is on a home-grade setup: a small office or workshop with a kitchenette, a
reception area, staff who make their own coffee rather than being served it, a business with
customers waiting on site.

## Why the meeting is physical

The sales motion is a salesperson driving out, setting the machine up, brewing a cup and letting
the room taste it. Two thirds of sales close within 24 hours of that meeting. This is why an
address someone can walk into is a hard requirement and why a pure e-commerce or remote-only
company is a weak lead even at the right headcount.

It also means the site needs somewhere to put a machine and someone on site during the day.

## What the booker offers on the call

A free visit to brew coffee, no obligation. "The worst that can happen is that you get a great cup
of coffee." Qualifying questions on the call are how many people are on site, what the current
machine and supplier are, how old it is, and who can sign. Those four questions are a good guide
to what a lead row should make possible: the row should tell the booker enough to ask them well.

## Reading a candidate

Good sign: 3 to 50 on site, own premises, staff and often customers present during the day,
a current setup that is obviously consumer-grade or shared.

Cafés, restaurants, bakeries and hotels stay in. Their guest-facing coffee is not the target, the
staff room is, and it is usually the most neglected setup in the building.

Out: coffee roasteries, coffee machine suppliers, vending and office drinks operators. They are
competitors, not prospects.
